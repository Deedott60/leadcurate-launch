WHERE I THINK I CAN HELP

Two moments I keep coming back to.

I do not know what is already covered in Gaston, and I would rather ask than assume. These are the two places my own experience is worth something.

Before. Holding a conversation with a stranger who has no reason to talk to you is a skill, and it can be practiced. It decides whether somebody gets past a screener, past a gatekeeper, and past a first impression.

After. The first ninety days have their own demands. What ramp means. How to take correction without shutting down. What a one on one is. What to do when you are behind and know it. Staying in the job is its own skill, and it can be supported.

One thing built, two things proposed

I want to be clear about which is which.

01Workshop series BUILT AND READY. Group sessions, in person, on site. 6 to 20 participants. OPEN

This part is finished. Eight sessions, written out, with participant materials and instructor notes. Each session ends with something the participant keeps: a written introduction, a rehearsed practice conversation, an offer review checklist, a ninety day plan. You are welcome to read any of it before deciding anything.

Professional communication

Opening a conversation with someone who is not expecting it. Reading a reaction and adjusting. Asking a question and actually listening to the answer. Talking about yourself for thirty seconds without a script.

Positioning your history

Turning warehouse, trade, kitchen, driving and crew work into evidence an employer can read. Handling gaps plainly. What an automated screening system reads and what it ignores.

The hiring process, start to finish

Who you meet, what each person is listening for, what to ask them, and what to send afterward.

The record conversation

When it comes up, who you tell, and how to answer in thirty seconds without flinching. Practiced until it does not shake them.

Human versus automated

Where a person can talk their way in, and where a system decides before anyone sees them. This changes where somebody applies.

After the offer

Reading an offer letter. What happens between signing and the first day. What orientation is. That feeling lost for two weeks is normal.

The first ninety days

What is expected at thirty, sixty and ninety days. Taking coaching without taking it personally. Asking for help before somebody offers it. Working with a supervisor and a team.

Current workplace tools

The software a workplace expects, including AI tools, used honestly and with verification. Addresses being behind on technology at the moment of hire.

Delivery options: a full eight session series, a condensed half day with hands on practice, or a single ninety minute session for a cohort or for staff.

02Individual coaching PROPOSED. One to one, during the series or after it. OPEN

I have not run this as a formal service and I would want to shape it with you. What I picture:

  • Practice conversations, repeated until steady
  • Rehearsing the record conversation for a specific employer
  • Preparing for a specific interview, including researching that employer beforehand
  • Reviewing what happened after an interview and adjusting
03Retention support PROPOSED. The first ninety days after somebody starts. OPEN

This is the piece I am least sure how to structure, and the one I think matters most. I have lived the first ninety days on the other side of a hire. I have not built a program around it. What I picture:

  • Scheduled check ins across the first ninety days
  • Working through a specific problem with a supervisor or a coworker
  • Coaching before and after a performance conversation with their employer
  • Helping the person communicate a problem early rather than going quiet
  • Documenting their own accomplishments as they happen

Going with somebody to an interview or an employer meeting is something I would do if it is wanted and the employer is comfortable with it. I understand that is already common practice in supported employment.

WHY THIS, FROM ME

The same company hired me for a professional role and turned me down for a delivery role. For the delivery job, a system decided and I never spoke to anybody.

Three employers ran a background check and hired anyway. Every time, the difference was getting in front of a person and being able to hold the conversation. That is where the workshop came from.

What I would document, and what I will not claim

I would report

  • Attendance by session
  • Completion
  • Deliverables each participant produced
  • Practice conversations completed and observed
  • Individual coaching sessions delivered
  • Retention check ins, and at what interval after hire
  • Participant self assessment against each session's stated objectives

I will not claim

  • Placement rates
  • Wage outcomes
  • Recidivism effects

Those depend on employers, the labor market and the individual. Promising them would not be honest.

What this is not

Stated plainly so there is no misunderstanding later.

Not case management

Housing, transportation, documents, benefits and treatment stay with the agency.

Not job placement

No employer relationships are promised or implied.
