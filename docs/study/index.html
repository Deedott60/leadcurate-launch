1. Investor Types — who buys our lists

Wholesaler
Buys a contract to purchase at a discount, then assigns that contract to another investor for a fee. Never owns the property. Our #1 customer type.
Fix-and-Flip Investor
Buys distressed properties, rehabs, sells retail. Needs ARV close to retail, repair estimate, and motivated sellers.
Buy-and-Hold Investor / Landlord
Buys rental properties for monthly cash flow. Cares about cap rate, NOI, neighborhood class.
BRRRR Investor
BRRRR = Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. Buys distressed, fixes it up, rents it out, refinances to pull cash back out, buys the next one.
Acquisitions Manager
Person at a real estate company whose job is finding deals. Buys lists, runs cold calls, hires VAs. Decision-maker for buying our service.
Hedge Fund Buyer / Institutional
Large company buying hundreds of homes (Invitation Homes, Tricon). Buys at scale, less price-sensitive. Different sales motion.
iBuyer
Opendoor, Offerpad. Companies that buy direct via algorithm. Compete with wholesalers for the same leads.
Bird Dog
Someone who finds leads for a wholesaler in exchange for a small fee per deal. Beginner role.

2. List Types — Industry Standard

Tax Delinquent List
Property owners behind on county property taxes. The classic motivated-seller list. Every wholesaler chases these.
Pre-Foreclosure List
Properties where foreclosure has been filed but not yet completed. Owner under pressure to sell.
Notice of Default (NOD) List
First step in foreclosure — bank files NOD when owner is behind on mortgage. Public record. Early-stage motivated lead.
Notice of Trustee Sale (NTS) / Lis Pendens
Auction scheduled. Owner has weeks left. Very motivated but very short window.
Tax Lien / Tax Deed Sale List
Properties going to auction for unpaid taxes. Different states use lien vs deed sales — research yours.
Sheriff Sale / Trustee Sale
Public auction of foreclosed property. List published before sale.
Probate List
Deceased owners' properties. Heirs want to settle the estate fast. Standard wholesaling list.
Absentee Owner List
Owner's mailing address ≠ property address. They're a landlord or out-of-area owner. Out-of-state absentee is the strongest filter.
High Equity / Free-and-Clear List
Owners with little or no mortgage. Can sell quickly without bank approval. Common request.
Vacant Property List
USPS-flagged vacant homes. Owner may not even live nearby. Industry term: "vacant filter."
Code Violation List
Properties with city code violations — distressed signal. Owner often overwhelmed.
Lien List (Mechanics / Judgment / HOA)
Properties with legal claims attached. Can't sell clean without paying them off.
Divorce List
Properties tied to a divorce filing. Couple needs to split assets — often motivated to sell fast.
Eviction / Tired Landlord List
Landlords with recent eviction filings. Burned out, want out.
Stacked List
A list filtered for MULTIPLE distress signals (e.g. absentee + tax delinquent + lien). Industry gold. Higher quality, smaller volume.
Cash Buyer List
People who bought property with cash recently. Used to find OTHER investors to wholesale TO, not from.

3. Deal Terms — How Investors Talk

Off-Market
Property not listed on MLS. Where wholesalers/investors find their best deals.
MLS (Multiple Listing Service)
The agent database. "On-market" = listed there. Investors generally avoid these — too competitive.
FSBO (For Sale By Owner)
Owner selling without an agent. Often open to creative offers.
Subject-To / Sub-To
Buying a property by taking over the existing mortgage payments without formally assuming the loan. Creative finance technique.
Seller Finance / Owner Carry
Seller acts as the bank. Buyer makes payments to them, not a lender.
Lease Option / Rent-to-Own
Tenant rents with an option to buy at a set price later. Used when buyers can't qualify for a loan today.
Assignment Contract / Assignment Fee
Wholesaler's contract that lets them assign their right to buy to another investor. Their profit = the assignment fee.
Wholetail
Hybrid wholesale-retail. Wholesaler does light cleanup and lists on MLS to capture more profit than a pure assignment.
REO (Real Estate Owned)
Bank-owned property — they foreclosed and now own it. Different list, different sales process.
Short Sale
Bank agrees to accept less than the mortgage balance. Owner under water but bank approval slow.
Distressed Property / Motivated Seller
Generic terms. Distressed = property has issues. Motivated seller = owner has personal reasons to move fast. Often go together.

4. Money / Returns — The Math

ARV — After Repair Value
ARV What the property would sell for after it's fixed up. Critical number.
MAO — Maximum Allowable Offer
MAO Most a wholesaler will pay and still make money.

70% Rule: (ARV × 0.70) − Repairs = MAO
Comps / Comparables
Recently sold similar properties. Used to establish ARV.
Cap Rate
For rentals. Annual NOI ÷ Property price. Buy-and-hold investors care about this.
NOI — Net Operating Income
Annual rental income minus operating expenses (not mortgage).
Cash on Cash Return
Annual cash flow ÷ cash invested. Different math than cap rate, accounts for the mortgage.
Hard Money / Private Money
Hard money: short-term loan from a company at high interest (10-15%). For flips.
Private money: loan from an individual investor. Cheaper, relationship-based.
DSCR Loan
Debt Service Coverage Ratio loan. Qualifies based on property income, not personal income. Popular for landlords.
1031 Exchange
Sell a rental, buy another within 180 days, defer capital gains taxes. Buy-and-hold strategy.

5. Legal & Title

Title Search
Public records search to verify ownership and find liens. Required before closing.
Clouded Title
Title with unresolved issues — lien, missing heir, old mortgage. Property can't sell clean.
Quitclaim vs Warranty Deed
Quitclaim: transfers whatever interest seller has — no guarantees. Used for fast transfers between known parties.
Warranty: seller guarantees clean title.
Earnest Money
Buyer's good-faith deposit when signing the contract. Usually $500-$5,000.
Escrow / Closing
Neutral third party holds money + docs until both sides perform. Then closes the deal.
Lis Pendens
Latin for "lawsuit pending." Public notice attached to a property. Foreclosure or other legal action filed.
DNC — Do Not Call Registry
Federal list. Calling someone on it without consent = fines. Our lists are address-only so DNC is the customer's responsibility.
TCPA
Telephone Consumer Protection Act. Federal law on cold calls and texts. Customer compliance, not ours.

6. Data Terms — What's Inside The List

Parcel ID / APN — Assessor's Parcel Number
Unique county ID for the land. Used to look up everything in public records.
Situs Address
The actual property address. "Situs" = Latin for "location."
Mailing Address
Where the owner receives mail. Could differ from situs (absentee owners).
Market Value / Assessed Value
Market value: what it's worth.
Assessed value: what the county uses to calculate taxes (often a % of market).
Entity vs Individual Owner
Entity-owned: LLC, corp, trust, REIT. Harder to reach.
Individual-owned: real person — can be called directly.
Skip Tracing
Finding phone numbers and email from a name and address. Services: BatchSkipTracing, REIPro, IDI, Skipify. Required if our customer wants to cold call.
Cost Per Lead (CPL) / Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)
CPL: what it costs to generate one lead (our list price ÷ records).
CPA: what it costs to acquire one CLOSED deal. Wholesalers track this religiously.

Our cloud database. Stores intake leads, prospects, messages, agent activity.

Scheduled task that runs automatically. Our daily Wake NC data pull is a cron.
Webhook

8. ⭐ Our 4-Tier Product System

Same data, four shelves — ranked by urgency. This is the framework that separates us from PropStream. Memorize these so you can recommend the right tier on a call.

Tier 1 — Hot Sheet (Auction Imminent)
Auction scheduled in next 30 days. Owner in full panic. $497/wk · 10–100 records · weekly refresh. Sell to fast-moving wholesalers who can contract this week.
Tier 2 — Fresh Triggers (Just Filed)
New court filing or code violation in last 7 days. Owner hasn't been pounded by competitors yet — first-mover advantage. $297/wk · 50–300 records · weekly refresh. Sell to daily cold callers.
Tier 3 — Breaking Point (About to Crack)
Debt growing year-over-year OR balance now exceeds 5% of property value. The math says they're about to lose it. $249/mo · 500–2,000 records · monthly refresh. Sell to buy-and-hold + flippers.
Tier 4 — Deep Distress (Steady-State)
The classic stacked-filter list. Tax Delinquent, Absentee, High Equity, Probate, Code Violation. $149/mo · 5K–10K records · monthly refresh. Sell to everyone — this is the baseline product.
The 30-second pitch: "We don't just look at how long a property has been on a list — we track its velocity. Someone owing $500 for three years isn't motivated. But our system flags the exact moment that debt hits a breaking point, or when the county officially schedules an auction date. You're not calling someone who's casually behind on taxes — you're calling someone who realizes they're about to lose their house next month. That's why our data closes deals while standard software lists just sit there."

9. ⚠ Our Internal Terms — Do NOT Use With Clients

These are LeadCurate-specific. If a wholesaler hears these, they won't know what you're talking about. Translate before speaking.

Permit Burnout Lane
What WE call it: properties with open damage permits
Say to clients: "list of properties with active fire/storm/damage permits — a code-violation adjacent list"
Lane
What WE call it: a specific filtered category within a market
Say to clients: "list type" or "stacked filter" or "segment"
Snapshot / Discovery Snapshot
What WE call it: a processed sellable batch
Say to clients: "pulled list" or "batch" or "delivery"
Seat
What WE call it: limited buyer access per county
Say to clients: "limited license" or "exclusive territory" or "we only sell this list to 1-3 buyers per market"
When marketing, you can use our terms in our copy. But when talking 1-on-1 with a serious investor, use industry language. That's how you sound credible.
