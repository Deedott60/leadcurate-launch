The three-county showdown

Where should your money actually go? Three finalists emerged from the scan.

The full ranking covers every county and municipality. These three tell the story, and the numbers below are computed straight from the parcel database, not estimated.

The Volume King

Middlesex County: the biggest bench in the state, at the steepest prices.

113,527 opportunity parcels, more than any other county. 90,965 tired landlords, 46,003 of whom have held 20+ years. 11,041 industrial/multifamily distress properties, also the state's largest. But the price of admission is real: median out-of-state-owned property runs $682,500, and median distressed industrial/multifamily runs $1,243,400. Boston-metro pricing compresses margins on every deal.

Our pick for you The Sweet Spot

Worcester County: second-deepest distress bench, at half the price.

63,386 opportunity parcels across 320,059 total. 8,192 industrial/multifamily distress properties, second in the state and the highest concentration of any large county at 256 per 10,000 parcels. 8,710 verified vacant parcels, also second statewide. 42,761 tired landlords, 23,696 at 20+ years. And the medians are where the deal math works: $284,300 for out-of-state-owned, $27,900 for vacant land, $408,900 for tired-landlord properties, $578,750 for distressed industrial/multifamily. Roughly half of Middlesex on every line, with real volume behind it.

Statewide lane detail

Your lanes across the whole state, before any geographic cut.

Tired Landlords

436,244 non-owner-occupied properties held 10+ years. Median hold: 20.5 years.

Massachusetts is a long-tenure state, and the median owner in this file has held since roughly 2005, before the last two price cycles. 37,395 of these owners live outside Massachusetts entirely. Median property value $541,400 statewide, but as the county table shows, the affordable end concentrates in Worcester, Hampden, and Bristol counties.

Industrial / Multifamily Distress

54,443 industrial and multifamily properties carrying an off-market signal.

23,476 industrial and 30,967 multifamily parcels where the owner is absentee or has held 10+ years. Median value $777,000 statewide, median hold 13 years, 7,529 owned from out of state.

Out-of-State Owners

115,764 Massachusetts properties owned from another state.

Median value $545,600, median hold 11.3 years. Cape Cod (Barnstable County) alone carries 24,880 of them, second-home country, a different conversation than the urban investor product but real volume if that lane ever interests you.

Verified Vacant Land

60,876 parcels verified as genuinely vacant. Median land value $33,300.

Same verification standard as every market we run: no hidden building value, classification supports vacant, usable acreage, an owner who can sell. 8,745 are owned from out of state, and the median owner has held 15 years.

Pre-foreclosure and tax-lien records in Massachusetts run through the Registry of Deeds, Land Court, and municipal tax-title processes rather than the statewide assessor database this scan was built from. Executive insights

What the numbers actually say about your home state.

Worcester is the price-to-depth winner. It carries the state's highest big-county concentration of distressed industrial/multifamily (256 per 10,000 parcels) at medians roughly half of the Boston metro. Volume without the margin squeeze.
Springfield is the density anomaly. One in three parcels in the entire city held 20+ years by a non-occupant owner is not a normal market condition. Marketing dollars go further there than anywhere else in the state.
The 20-year cohort is enormous. Statewide median hold in the tired-landlord file is 20.5 years, meaning half of 436,244 owners predate the 2008 crash entirely. That cohort has equity, fatigue, and no memory of current pricing, the exact profile that sells off-market.
Middlesex is where you go second, not first. The bench is deepest but the entry prices demand bigger capital per deal. Win in Worcester or Springfield, then scale up.
