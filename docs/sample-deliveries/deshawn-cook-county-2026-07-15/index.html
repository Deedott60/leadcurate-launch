Reading Cook County values correctly

One thing to know before the numbers: Illinois reports assessed value, not market value.

Cook County's files carry the county's assessed values, which by Illinois's classification system are a statutory fraction of what a property would actually trade for. So when a median below reads $52,000, that is the assessment figure, not the street price, and actual market values run several times higher. We report the county's own numbers rather than inflating them with estimates. Your delivered file carries the assessment class per parcel so market value can be reasoned per property class.

Industrial / Multifamily Distress

178,866 properties: 155,636 multifamily and 23,230 industrial, each carrying an off-market signal.

Chicago's housing stock is built on two-to-six-unit multifamily buildings, and 166,681 of these properties have owners who don't occupy them. Tired Landlords

84,780 non-owner-occupied properties held 10+ years. Median hold: 17.2 years.

16,196 of these are multifamily. Median assessed value $30,178. The median owner here bought around 2009, at the bottom of the crash, meaning this cohort is sitting on the largest equity gains in the county's recent history.

Out-of-State Owners

61,559 Cook County properties owned from outside Illinois.

Median hold 6.7 years, so like Dallas, this file skews toward recent buyers. The sharpest cut is the overlap: out-of-state AND long tenure, 2,702 owners, which your delivered file isolates directly.

Verified Vacant Land

2,918 parcels verified vacant under our standard. Honest note: this undercounts Chicago.

Cook County's assessment file classifies vacant land narrowly, and our verification only counts what the data can prove. The real vacant inventory in Chicago (including city-owned lots and side lots) lives in separate city datasets that can be added per order. We'd rather show you 2,918 real ones than 30,000 maybes.

Two of your lanes have a data-access reality in Cook County we won't paper over: the county's public tax-sale catalog is inactive (its last full list is from 2016), and the public recorder foreclosure feed stopped updating in 2015. Current tax-delinquency and foreclosure data exist but sit with the Treasurer and court systems rather than open data, and get sourced per order through those channels. Everything above is verified and deliverable today.
