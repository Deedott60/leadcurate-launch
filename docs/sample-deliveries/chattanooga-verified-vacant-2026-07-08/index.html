The actual question you asked

"What's the difference between your data and what I already have?"

Fair question. You didn't ask for a sales pitch, you asked what's different. Most vacant-land lists on the market get compiled once, sold many times, and never rechecked. How a parcel earns a spot on this list

About 1 in 8 parcels in the county actually qualify. Here's what knocks the rest out.

A "vacant" flag in a county file is a starting point, not proof. Before a parcel counts as sellable land here, it has to clear all six of these:

  1. Still vacant right now. Not vacant according to a record from a year ago.
  2. No hidden building value. A parcel can be labeled vacant land and still carry an improvement on the books. This one doesn't.
  3. The county's own numbers back it up. The land use classification actually supports "vacant," not an ambiguous or mislabeled parcel type.
  4. Real usable acreage. Not a leftover sliver, right-of-way remnant, or drainage strip too small to build on.
  5. An owner who can actually sell. Government, utility, church, HOA, and similar institutional owners are excluded. They almost never sell.
  6. Current ownership on file. So outreach lands on the person who actually owns it today.
