# LeadCurate Commercial Creative Brief

## 1. Reference Commercial Description

The referenced TMA Insurance Trust life-insurance ad effectively uses a **human-first approach** by starting with a relatable professional in a real-world context, avoiding generic imagery. It highlights a **clear pain point**—the perceived ease of online/self-service options contrasted with the actual friction users experience. The ad then offers a **personal alternative**, transitioning from a generic transaction to advisor-led assistance. The **calm voiceover** is realistic and trustworthy, steering clear of hype or overly technical demonstrations. **Subtitles/captions** are utilized to succinctly advance the narrative. The **pacing** is deliberately slow, allowing scenes to breathe and the message to resonate. It concludes with a strong **brand promise**: “served, not sold,” emphasizing a human touch.

## 2. What Made the Previous LeadCurate Attempt Feel Cheap/Garbage

The previous LeadCurate promos suffered from a **fast-paced, website walkthrough style**, lacking a narrative arc and emotional engagement. They focused too heavily on the mechanics, making them feel like a **tech demo** rather than a compelling story. The absence of a **clear pain point** and **personal connection** made them feel generic and unmemorable. **Rapid section transitions** and **choppy visuals** detracted from the premium feel, and the **voiceover** was overly technical and lacked warmth.

## 3. New Creative Direction for LeadCurate

The new LeadCurate commercial will adopt a **story-driven approach**, focusing on the buyer/acquisition professional's journey. It will highlight the **pain point** of dealing with stale/recycled records and introduce LeadCurate as a **deliberate, refined solution**. The narrative will be **human-centric**, with a focus on the professional's experience and the tangible benefits of using LeadCurate. The **visual style** will be cinematic, with slower shots and a focus on **subtle, impactful storytelling**. The commercial will conclude with a **premium brand lockup** and a restrained call-to-action.

## 4. 6-Scene Outline with Exact Pacing and Shot Purpose

1. **Scene 1 (5 seconds):** A buyer/acquisition professional sits at their desk, looking frustrated with a stack of stale records.  
   *Purpose:* Establish the relatable pain point.

2. **Scene 2 (5 seconds):** Close-up of the professional's face, conveying determination to find a better solution.  
   *Purpose:* Human connection and emotional engagement.

3. **Scene 3 (5 seconds):** Introduction of LeadCurate on a computer screen, highlighting the clean and intuitive interface.  
   *Purpose:* Present LeadCurate as the solution.

4. **Scene 4 (7 seconds):** The process unfolds visually: Source → Refine → Prioritize → Reserve, with subtle animations.  
   *Purpose:* Show the streamlined process without over-explaining.

5. **Scene 5 (5 seconds):** Briefly showcase a tangible example batch, emphasizing quality and relevance.  
   *Purpose:* Make the solution feel real and accessible.

6. **Scene 6 (8 seconds):** The professional smiles, satisfied with the results. End with a premium brand lockup and a simple, elegant CTA.  
   *Purpose:* Reinforce brand promise and encourage action.

## 5. Voiceover Principles

- **Tone:** Calm, measured, and trustworthy, avoiding any hint of hype or over-excitement.
- **Cadence:** Slow and deliberate, allowing each word to resonate and the viewer to absorb the message.
- **Forbidden Phrasing:** Avoid technical jargon, overly complex terms, and any language that feels like a sales pitch.
- **Key Focus:** Emphasize the human benefits and the ease of the solution, aligning with the narrative arc and emotional journey.
