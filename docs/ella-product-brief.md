# LeadCurate product brief for Ella

## Plain goal

LeadCurate is not trying to be a generic “we sell leads” company.

The goal is to become a premium county-based lead intelligence product for serious real estate investors by turning messy public/source records into cleaned, deduped, source-reviewed, scored, and buyer-ready lead batches.

The trust position matters: this should feel like a legitimate data operation, not a hype lead seller, spam shop, or “guaranteed motivated sellers” scam.

## What LeadCurate sells

LeadCurate sells access to reviewed county lead batches.

A batch should be positioned as:

- county-based
- limited-seat
- source-reviewed
- cleaned and deduped
- scored or prioritized
- delivered with decision context
- contact-enriched where available
- DNC-aware where applicable
- protected from being oversold during the buyer’s access/assignment period

Better public wording:

> Limited-seat county lead intelligence for serious investors.

Avoid making it sound like commodity list sales.

## What LeadCurate is not

LeadCurate is not:

- a guaranteed-deals company
- a “motivated seller guarantee” company
- a dialer, SMS blaster, or outreach service
- legal/compliance advice
- a promise that every record has contact data
- a promise that every record is safe to call or text
- a mass-resold generic list vendor

Customers handle outreach, compliance, negotiation, closing, and final verification.

Suggested compliance line:

> LeadCurate provides data and education only. Customers are responsible for outreach, compliance, verification, and closing.

## Why it is not a scam

The business should be built around proof and restraint:

1. We review source availability before selling seats.
2. We do not promise guaranteed deals.
3. We include source dates and decision context.
4. We limit buyer access by county/seat count instead of reselling the same file everywhere.
5. We mark contact/DNC information carefully, only where available/applicable.
6. We define replacement rules around data quality failures, not lack of seller motivation.
7. We keep auditability: where the record came from, when it was reviewed, why it made the batch.

## Cross-reference / verification logic

The product quality comes from cross-referencing and reviewing records before delivery.

Core checks should include:

- source availability by county
- record volume by county
- source date or last-review date
- duplicate removal
- obvious bad-record removal
- territory/county fit
- ownership/property matching where possible
- lead scoring or priority reason
- contact enrichment where available
- DNC-aware fields where applicable
- assignment/reservation protection so the same record is not repeatedly sold during an active access window

This is the moat: LeadCurate should feel like a curated intelligence batch, not a scraped spreadsheet.

## What customers receive

The landing page should clearly say what a customer actually receives.

Example deliverables:

- cleaned county file
- CSV/XLSX delivery
- source date or review date
- score or priority reason
- assignment ID
- contact data where available
- DNC-aware fields where applicable
- monthly or biweekly delivery cadence depending on plan/source activity
- replacement review for records that fail stated quality standards

## County review / funnel

The “Check county availability” step is not just a contact form. It is the beginning of the product.

Suggested copy:

> We review source availability, usable record volume, and available buyer seat count before activation.

Primary CTA:

> Check county availability

Secondary CTA:

> View pricing

Avoid too many CTA variations like Apply, Request quote, Reserve review, Check My County, etc. Pick one main phrase and use it consistently.

## Seat limits / exclusivity

LeadCurate should not imply every county is fully exclusive unless we define that and price it correctly.

Use safer wording:

- limited-seat county access
- preferred access option
- available buyer seats depend on source volume and usable lead count
- some counties may only support one buyer
- records assigned to an active buyer are protected during the active access period

FAQ questions to answer:

- Why are seats limited?
- What does the county review include?
- What happens if a county only supports one buyer?
- Is this exclusive?
- Do you provide the same records to everyone?

## Landing page priority edits

Do not redesign from scratch. Tighten copy and structure.

Top changes:

1. Rework hero around “limited-seat county lead intelligence.”
2. Add a “What you receive” section with concrete deliverables.
3. Add FAQ/compliance block.
4. Add “where available / where applicable” around contact and DNC language.
5. Make the county review feel like a real evaluation.
6. Standardize CTAs around “Check county availability” and “View pricing.”

## Commercial / visual direction

The video should not feel like a website walkthrough.

It should feel like a realistic commercial:

- female professional / advisor / real estate operations person
- real work-from-home or small-office desk
- laptop, tablet, second monitor, property records, county maps, notes, coffee/water
- believable working environment, not fake luxury stock-photo office
- calm voiceover
- problem → reviewed data process → clean batch → limited access → CTA

Tone: premium, honest, competent, serious, not flashy.

## Short positioning summary

LeadCurate helps serious investors avoid generic stale lists by reviewing county source availability, refining property-owner records, adding decision context, and delivering limited-seat lead batches with source dates and compliance-aware fields where available.
