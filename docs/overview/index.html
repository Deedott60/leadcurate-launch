01Getting In Three sessions · from not knowing these jobs exist to sitting in a real interview SESSIONS 1–3
01

The field and the fit

What these roles actually are and what each one does on a Tuesday. How a sales paycheck is really built, including the retention check most people have never heard of. Which doors are open. Then an honest conversation about whether this is for them.

WALKS OUT WITH

A map of the roles and a shortlist that fits them.

02

Positioning yourself

Finding the evidence already in their history. Running a crew, dealing with homeowners, handling people at the worst moment of their day. And the part nobody tells them: many of these jobs sell to the service industry, so knowing what a roofer charges a square is knowledge the person next to them does not have.

WALKS OUT WITH

A sales resume, a thirty second introduction they can say out loud, and a professional profile.

03

The interview chain

Who they will actually meet and what each person is listening for. Three questions to ask, and why asking nothing is disqualifying. Then the exercise unique to sales hiring: the mock cold call, run live.

WALKS OUT WITH

Prep for each stage, a scored mock call, and a follow-up ready to send.

02Offer to Day One One session · offer letter to first daySESSION 4
04

The part nobody explains

Reading an offer line by line. What a draw is. When benefits start. How retention changes what you get paid. Then the onboarding sequence: equipment arriving at the house, logins to systems they have never seen, meeting the team on video before the start date, and what ramp will expect.

Why it exists: people make it through interviews and still wash out here, because nobody told them what any of it means or that feeling lost for two weeks is normal.

WALKS OUT WITH

An offer review checklist and the questions worth asking before accepting.

03The Job Itself Three sessions · the actual work, from the morning routine to the close SESSIONS 5–7
05

The tools and the day

The vocabulary of the systems they will meet on day one. What a rep actually looks up before dialing. How a pipeline works and what happens when it is not kept clean. Then building a working day with real time blocks.

WALKS OUT WITH

Their own daily rhythm, written and defended out loud.

06

Prospecting and the opening call

Choosing who to call instead of dialing a list. Working with a gatekeeper instead of against one. The opener, and reading what kind of call it actually is before saying anything else. Then discovery, and letting somebody talk long enough to tell you the answer themselves.

WALKS OUT WITH

A researched prospecting sheet, an opener in their own words, and a set of discovery questions.

07

Presenting, objections, price and closing

Handing back what you heard instead of giving a product tour. The four objections that actually come up, in the customer's real words. Delivering a price without apologizing for it. And what to do when somebody gets cold feet after they have already said yes.

WALKS OUT WITH

A short presentation, an objection sheet in their own wording, and a customer handoff document.

04First 90 Days and Moving Up One session · staying employed long enough to get goodSESSION 8
08

Surviving and advancing

What ramp expects at thirty, sixty and ninety days. Reading your own numbers and finding your own weak spot before a manager finds it for you. What a coaching meeting is and how to show up to one. What actually gets people cut, which is almost never talent. Then where the career goes from here.

WALKS OUT WITH

A ninety day plan and a record of their own work, started in the room.

THE IDEA UNDERNEATH

This is not a course about sales. It is a rehearsal of being onboarded into a job, run start to finish, so the real version is recognizable instead of overwhelming.

What makes it different

It keeps going after the interview

The offer, the paperwork, the first day and the first ninety days are all part of the course, because that is the stretch where people wash out.

It came from real calls

The objections are in the customer's actual words. The pricing lessons come from real accounts, including the ones that went wrong. It is not theory rewritten from a book.

AI is taught inside the work

No separate AI class. They use it to research a business before calling, build a list, and practice against something that will not hang up on them. Every time, they verify what it gave them. The selling is still theirs.

They carry a playbook

Not a reference manual. A book built to sit open during a live call so somebody can find their place when they realize they skipped a step. That is how working reps actually use one.

How it can run

Pacing built around the center's calendar and its participants, not a fixed template.

Full course

All eight sessions, in sequence, with the practice and the materials that go with each one.

Half day

A condensed version with hands on keyboards. They build a prospecting sheet and run one live practice call.

Ninety minutes

A first look for staff or a single group. Enough for a room to see what the full thing is.
