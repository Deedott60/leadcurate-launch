# LeadCurate — 30–35 Second Commercial Production Package

---

## Recommended Voiceover Script (Script A)

> *Most records have already been worked. Called, pitched, passed over. Buyers know the feeling — chasing the same names everyone else is chasing. LeadCurate changes that. It sources, refines, and prioritizes records before they reach your team — so every list you work feels like it was built for you. Fresh. Relevant. Ready.*
>
> **LeadCurate. Work better records.**

**Word count:** 72 words
**Recommended:** ✅ Yes — this script earns trust through restraint. It names the pain without dramatizing it, explains the solution without demoing it, and closes with a brand line that is confident without being loud.

## Voice Direction

- **Talent profile:** Male or female, 35–50, measured and grounded — think public radio narrator, not ad announcer
- **Pace:** Approximately 130–140 words per minute; never rushed
- **Tone:** Conversational authority — the voice of someone who has seen the problem and quietly solved it
- **Delivery notes:** Slight pause after "chasing the same names everyone else is chasing." Let the silence do work. The brand line at the end should land softly, not as a punchline
- **Avoid:** Rising inflection on any line; breathless energy; anything that sounds like a pitch

---

## Music Direction

- **Style:** Sparse, modern ambient — single piano motif or light acoustic guitar with subtle electronic texture underneath
- **Reference feel:** Nils Frahm, Ólafur Arnalds, or early Jon Hopkins instrumental work
- **Dynamics:** Begins at -18 dB under voiceover; swells gently under Scene 4 process sequence; resolves cleanly under brand lockup
- **Tempo:** No discernible beat or driving rhythm — the music breathes with the edit, not against it
- **Avoid:** Uplifting corporate lift music, any percussion that drives urgency, anything that signals "product demo"

### Scene 1 — 5 Seconds
**Purpose:** Establish the relatable pain point

**Prompt:**
> Over-the-shoulder shot of a professional at a clean wooden desk, late afternoon natural window light. Their right hand rests on a printed spreadsheet, partially crumpled. A second sheet sits beside it, marked up with a pen. The camera slowly pushes in from behind the left shoulder. No face visible. The desk is organized but tired — a half-empty coffee cup, a sticky note with a crossed-out name. Cinematic color grade, warm shadows, shallow depth of field. Shot on 35mm. Slow, deliberate motion.

**On-Screen Copy:**
> *"The same records. Again."*

**Negative Prompt:**
> faces, smiling, bright office lighting, stock photo aesthetic, fast motion, zoom effects, text overlays with multiple lines, cluttered backgrounds, neon, blue tones, corporate cheerfulness, lens flare, motion blur

- **The camera never rushes.** Every scene is designed to hold — a slow push, a static frame, a deliberate pause. The previous version's rapid transitions signaled anxiety; this version signals confidence. Premium brands do not rush their audience.
