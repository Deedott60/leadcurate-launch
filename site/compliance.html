Compliance & Acceptable Use

Last updated: [INSERT DATE]

LeadCurate provides curated property-record data sourced from public records. Our customers use this data for real estate research and outreach. Outreach is your responsibility, and so is compliance with the laws that govern it. This page summarizes the most important rules. It is not legal advice.

If you cold-call, text, or send mail without complying with these laws, you can face statutory damages of $500–$1,500 per call or message under the TCPA, plus penalties under state laws. Take this seriously.

1. TCPA (Federal — Phone & Text)

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) restricts unsolicited calls and texts to consumers. Key requirements:

  • Do not auto-dial or robocall consumer cell phones without prior express consent.
  • Do not send marketing texts without prior express written consent.
  • Do not call numbers on the federal Do-Not-Call (DNC) registry without an established business relationship or written consent.
  • Identify yourself and your company at the start of every call.
  • Honor opt-out requests immediately.
  • Calling hours are restricted (generally 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local time of the called party).

2. Do-Not-Call (DNC) Registries

You must scrub your call list against both the federal DNC registry and any applicable state DNC list before calling. LeadCurate flags publicly available DNC indicators where possible, but you are responsible for performing a current scrub of your own before each calling session.

3. State Telemarketing & Privacy Laws

Many states have additional rules. Examples include Florida’s Telephone Solicitation Act (which is stricter than the TCPA), Oklahoma’s Consumer Protection Act, and Washington’s Commercial Electronic Mail Act. Check the laws of every state you are calling into.

4. State Real Estate & Wholesaling Laws

Wholesaling is increasingly regulated. North Carolina, South Carolina, Oklahoma, Illinois, and other states have passed or are considering wholesaler-disclosure or wholesaler-licensing laws. Know the rules in any state where you contract or assign properties.

5. CAN-SPAM (Email)

If you send marketing email, you must include a valid physical postal address, a clear unsubscribe link, and accurate sender information. Honor opt-outs within 10 business days.

6. Fair Housing Act

Do not target or exclude property owners based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability. This applies to your outreach scripts, ad copy, and targeting criteria.

7. Skip-Trace Data Use Limits

Skip-traced phone numbers and email addresses provided in LeadCurate batches:

  • Are not consent records. Their presence in your file does not give you consent to call or text.
  • May be incorrect, outdated, or reassigned. Verify before contacting.
  • Must not be used to harass, stalk, or threaten any person.
  • Must not be resold, listed, or redistributed.

8. What LeadCurate Does and Does Not Do

We do: review county source records, clean and dedupe data, attach source dates, flag publicly available DNC indicators where possible, and provide call-prep and offer-review materials.

We do not: guarantee accuracy of DNC flags, provide legal advice, certify compliance with any law, perform real-time DNC scrubs at the moment you call, or take responsibility for your outreach decisions.

9. Reporting a Concern

If you believe LeadCurate data is being misused, report it to abuse@leadcurate.com. We investigate every report and may terminate accounts found in violation of these rules.

10. Property-Owner Removal Requests

If you are a property owner who does not wish to have your information appear in LeadCurate’s database, email privacy@leadcurate.com with the property address. We will remove your information from our internal database within 30 days. Note that we cannot alter or delete the underlying public records maintained by your county.
